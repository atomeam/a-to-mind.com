# F-301 Sonar Bat Cartographer (2026-10-28)
**Combination:** echolocation as the only way to perceive, plus on-device tabular Q-learning, plus stereo Web Audio sonar, plus a map that exists only as fading echo memory, plus a HUD that bends with the agent's surprise (TD error). The ledger had no earlier sonar, echolocation or Q-learning entries. F-299 Hum Crystals was about sound input; this one uses sound as a sense for a learning agent.
**Architecture:** `drafts/F301-sonar-bat-cartographer/sonar-core.js` holds the shared cave generator (cellular automaton plus largest-region flood fill, seeded xorshift), five echo rays, moth line-of-sight bearing, and a 2187-state × 3-action Q-table. It runs in both the browser and Node. `index.html` has a Canvas2D echo-memory renderer, Web Audio chirps (delay and pitch set by distance, pan set by ray angle), a Q-value bar HUD whose inner panel tilts, rounds and glows with |TD|, and a human "fly by ear" mode (arrows plus Space). The Q-table persists in localStorage, `?seed=` makes cave links shareable, and there are Train 20k, New cave and Forget buttons.
**Measured:** `node test.js` trains on 20 caves × 20k steps, then evaluates on 10 unseen caves × 4000 steps at ε=0.05. The learner caught 9.6 moths per cave against 0.5 for a random policy, and bumped walls 41 times against 418.7. Egocentric echo perception carries over to new caves. In headless Chrome at 390×800 there were no page errors, and 20k training steps took about 22 ms.
**Failure modes and lessons:** In the first build the whole HUD wobbled with surprise, so the buttons never held still and Playwright could not click them. That is also an accessibility bug. Now only the inner "mind" panel moves, the controls stay still, and prefers-reduced-motion turns the motion off. On a portrait phone the landscape cave sits under the panel, so tapping the title folds it. Line-of-sight moth sensing is a cheat compared with real sonar.
**Next building blocks:** Swap the Q-table for a tiny on-device neural net over raw echo distances. Render the echo memory in 3D or WebXR. Use real microphone clicks from the user as the bat's chirp. Let several bats share one Q-table, a swarm cartographer that builds a shared map.
